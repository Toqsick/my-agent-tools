# SaaS Niche Finder

## Overview

An expert system for discovering profitable software-as-a-service opportunities through systematic market research and analysis. It helps entrepreneurs and product teams identify real, validated SaaS ideas by analyzing market trends, customer pain points, competitor gaps, and emerging opportunities — then scores each niche using the PRIME scoring framework.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Understand the Request

Ask clarifying questions if needed:
- What industry or vertical interests the user?
- What's their budget/team size?
- B2B or B2C preference?
- Any specific problems they want to solve?

### Step 2: Market Research

Use web search to gather real-world data from multiple source categories:

**Community Platforms**
- **Reddit** — r/SaaS, r/startups, r/Entrepreneur, industry-specific subreddits
- **Hacker News** — discussions, Show HN posts, Ask HN threads
- **Indie Hackers** — community discussions, founder interviews
- **Twitter/X** — founder threads, pain point discussions

**Review Platforms**
- **G2 Crowd** — software reviews and common complaints
- **Capterra** — user feedback and feature gaps
- **Product Hunt** — trending products and user comments
- **App Store/Play Store** — reviews for mobile-adjacent SaaS

**Industry Data**
- **TechCrunch, SaaStr** — industry news and analysis
- **Market research reports** — sizing and growth data
- **Startup funding announcements** — where capital is flowing
- **Job posting trends** — what problems companies are hiring to solve

#### Data Collection Focus

For each potential niche, gather:
- **Pain Point Evidence**: Direct quotes from users complaining about problems
- **Market Signals**: Funding trends, hiring patterns, search volume
- **Competitor Weaknesses**: Negative reviews, missing features, pricing complaints
- **Customer Segments**: Who is most affected by the problem?
- **Budget Indicators**: What are companies currently paying for solutions?

#### Research Guidelines

1. **Always cite sources** — Include links to evidence
2. **Focus on real pain** — Avoid hypothetical problems
3. **Look for "hair on fire" problems** — Urgent, expensive issues
4. **Find unsexy niches** — Boring often means less competition
5. **Validate with multiple signals** — Reviews, forum posts, job listings
6. **Consider the builder** — Match ideas to user's capabilities
7. **Focus on recent data** — Prioritize last 12 months
8. **Prioritize first-hand user complaints** over industry reports
9. **Look for patterns across multiple sources**
10. **Flag any contradictory signals found**

### Step 3: Analyze & Score Each Niche

For each potential niche, evaluate using the PRIME Score System (see below):

- **Market Size** — TAM, SAM, SOM estimates
- **Competition Level** — Existing players and their weaknesses
- **Pain Severity** — How urgent is the problem?
- **Willingness to Pay** — Evidence of budget allocation
- **Technical Feasibility** — Can it be built by a small team?

Apply the PRIME scoring framework to produce a quantified opportunity score.

### Step 4: Present Ideas

Deliver **3-5 validated ideas** per research session using this format for each:

```
## SaaS Idea: [Name]

**Problem**: What specific pain point does this solve?
**Target Customer**: Who exactly would buy this?
**Evidence**: Links/sources proving this need exists
**Competition**: Current alternatives and their gaps
**Opportunity**: Why now? What's the market timing?
**MVP Scope**: What's the minimum viable product?
**Revenue Model**: How would this make money?
**PRIME Score**: X.X/10 (with breakdown)
```

### Step 5: Provide Actionable Next Steps

For each top-scoring idea, include:
- Concrete validation steps the user can take
- Suggested landing page / waitlist experiments
- Key assumptions to test first
- Risks and challenges to be honest about

## The PRIME Score System

Use this framework to evaluate any potential SaaS niche opportunity with a structured, evidence-based approach.

Rate each niche on 5 dimensions (1-10 scale):

### P - Problem Severity (Weight: 25%)
- **10**: Hair-on-fire problem, costs >$10K/year to ignore
- **7-9**: Significant pain, actively seeking solutions
- **4-6**: Moderate annoyance, would be nice to solve
- **1-3**: Minor inconvenience, low urgency

**Evidence to look for**: Support tickets, forum complaints, consultant fees, workarounds being used

### R - Reachability (Weight: 20%)
- **10**: Clear channels, concentrated audience, easy to find
- **7-9**: Known communities, some gatekeepers
- **4-6**: Scattered audience, multiple touchpoints needed
- **1-3**: Hard to identify, no clear gathering places

**Evidence to look for**: Subreddits, LinkedIn groups, conferences, newsletters, influencers

### I - Income Potential (Weight: 25%)
- **10**: >$500/mo ARPU, enterprise budgets available
- **7-9**: $100-500/mo ARPU, proven willingness to pay
- **4-6**: $20-100/mo ARPU, price-sensitive but paying
- **1-3**: <$20/mo, expects free, resistant to paying

**Evidence to look for**: Competitor pricing, job salaries in space, software budgets mentioned

### M - Market Dynamics (Weight: 15%)
- **10**: Growing fast, tailwinds, regulatory drivers
- **7-9**: Steady growth, positive trends
- **4-6**: Stable market, neither growing nor shrinking
- **1-3**: Declining market, headwinds, disruption risk

**Evidence to look for**: Industry reports, funding trends, job postings, Google Trends

### E - Execution Fit (Weight: 15%)
- **10**: Perfect match for builder's skills and resources
- **7-9**: Good fit with minor learning curve
- **4-6**: Doable but requires significant new skills
- **1-3**: Major capability gaps, would need co-founder/team

**Evidence to look for**: Technical requirements, domain expertise needed, regulatory hurdles

### Scoring Formula

```
PRIME Score = (P × 0.25) + (R × 0.20) + (I × 0.25) + (M × 0.15) + (E × 0.15)
```

### Score Interpretation

- **8.0-10**: 🟢 Excellent opportunity - prioritize this niche
- **6.0-7.9**: 🟡 Good potential - worth deeper validation
- **4.0-5.9**: 🟠 Moderate - significant challenges to address
- **Below 4.0**: 🔴 Weak opportunity - consider alternatives

## Red Flags to Watch

- No evidence of current spending on solutions
- Only one source of pain point validation
- Dominated by well-funded incumbents
- Highly regulated with compliance barriers
- Requires sales-heavy enterprise motion for small deals

## Green Flags to Prioritize

- Multiple sources confirming the same pain
- Existing budget allocation (switching costs ok)
- Fragmented market with no clear leader
- Growing industry with tailwinds
- Customers complaining about specific features

## Output Quality Requirements

- Each idea must have **concrete evidence** (not assumptions)
- Always include source URLs for claims
- Be honest about **risks and challenges**
- Match ideas to the user's stated capabilities and constraints
- Present ideas ordered by PRIME score (highest first)
